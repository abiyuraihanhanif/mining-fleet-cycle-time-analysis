06Key Insights
Payload gap

Trucks are running 18–26% under the 95t payload target

Even excluding zero-ton cycles, average loaded payload is 87.3t for PC1250 and 97.3t for PC2000 — but once the 19.4% zero-ton cycles are folded in, blended averages fall to 70.5t and 78.3t. PC1250's shortfall is the larger of the two once zero-tons are included, driven almost entirely by those empty cycles rather than under-filling on the loads that did happen.

Anomaly

1 in 5 truck cycles hauls zero tons — this is the single largest productivity leak

32 of 165 cycles show a complete truck-excavator cycle (queue, spot, load, haul, dump) with 0 tons recorded. If these cycles had carried the average loaded payload instead, the shift would have moved roughly 2,960 additional tons — about 24% more than the 12,311 tons actually recorded. This looks less like a demand issue and more like a data-capture or dispatch-matching issue (e.g. trucks cycling through without a valid load reading) and is worth validating against the source FMS/dispatch log.

Cycle time

PC2000 cycles run ~63% longer than PC1250, largely from haul distance, not loading

Average total cycle time is 20.8 min for PC1250 vs 33.9 min for PC2000. Loading itself is only ~2.4 min slower (4.75 vs 2.34 min), but full-travel duration is 6.9 min longer on average (16.8 vs 9.9 min) — PC2000 is evidently feeding dump points that sit further from the pit. Spot time also runs above plan for both models (PC1250: 1.56 vs 0.9 min target; PC2000: 2.15 vs 0.9 min target), a smaller but consistent gap worth a spotting-procedure review.

Utilization spread

Excavator workload is uneven: some units handle 6–9× more cycles than others

PC012 completed 18 cycles in the shift window vs 2 for PC028 and PC032. Some of this reflects genuinely shorter local routes (e.g. Load G/Dump G is the highest-volume route with the shortest full-travel time), but the spread is wide enough that it's worth checking whether it reflects deliberate dispatch strategy, downtime on the low-cycle units, or an assignment imbalance.

Peak load

15:00–16:00 is the congestion peak

Half of all recorded cycles (82 of 165) and over half the shift's tonnage fall in the 15:00 hour, with average queue time also peaking there (4.1 min vs a 0.96 min average the following hour). This is the hour where added queueing capacity or tighter truck-excavator matching would have the most impact.

07Recommendations
1
Audit the zero-tonnage cycles first.

Before optimizing anything else, confirm with the FMS/dispatch team whether these 32 cycles are genuine empty runs (e.g. relocation, standby) or a payload-sensor / data-linking fault. This single fix carries the largest potential tonnage upside in the dataset.

2
Investigate spotting time against plan for both excavator types.

Both PC1250 (+0.66 min) and PC2000 (+1.25 min) run over the spot-time target. A focused observation of the spotting approach at the higher-volume pits (Load F, G, H) could recover this gap fleet-wide.

3
Re-balance excavator-to-route assignment.

Route the shortest-haul pits (e.g. Load G↔Dump G, Load A↔Dump A) toward under-utilized units like PC028/PC032, and reserve the longer PC2000 hauls (Load E, F, D) for units already carrying volume efficiently, to close the utilization spread.

4
Add dispatch capacity for the 15:00 peak.

Queue time nearly quadruples in the peak hour versus the following one. Pre-positioning trucks or staggering assignments ahead of 15:00 should reduce this specific bottleneck.
